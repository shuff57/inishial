# Syllabus Gap Analysis

**Compared:** `Syllabus - Stats.docx` (PV High School, Intro to Statistics) vs. `Syllabus Example.pdf` (Delta College, BUS 3 Financial Accounting — Canvas syllabus page).

Since Stats is dual enrollment, it carries college obligations the example covers and Stats currently doesn't. Below: what's missing, in priority order, with drop-in text where it's cheap to write. `[College]` = whichever college grants the credit — swap in the real name and their real links before publishing.

---

## Tier 1 — Add these. Dual enrollment effectively requires them.

### 1. College course identity + official course description
The example leads with the college course number, title, and the articulation codes (`UC, CSU, C-ID ACCT 110`). A DE syllabus has to show the students what college course they're actually enrolled in, and the description usually must match the college catalog **verbatim** — that's the basis of the articulation.

> **[College] Course:** MATH [##] — Introduction to Statistics (4 units) · Transfers: CSU, UC · C-ID MATH 110
>
> **Course Description:** *[paste the catalog description verbatim from the college catalog]*
>
> Students in this course are earning both Pleasant Valley High School credit and [College] credit. The college grade earned becomes part of a permanent college transcript.

### 2. Disability accommodations (504/IEP **and** college DSPS)
Nothing in Stats. This is the biggest hole — it's an access-rights notice, and DE students have to register separately with the college's DSPS office; a high school IEP/504 does not automatically carry over.

> **Accommodations:** Students with a current IEP or 504 plan should see me in the first week so we can put supports in place. Because this course also carries [College] credit, accommodations on the college side are handled by [College] Disability Support Programs and Services (DSPS) — a high school IEP or 504 does not transfer automatically. Contact DSPS at [phone / email / link] to arrange college accommodations.

### 3. Drop / withdrawal dates and the "W"
The example spends a full section on census date, no-penalty drop date, "W" date, and who is responsible for dropping. Completely absent from Stats, and it's the single highest-stakes DE difference: a student who stops participating gets a W or an F on a **college transcript**, which follows them to financial aid and transfer.

> **Dropping the college course:** This class is on the high school schedule, but the college deadlines are separate and are not waived.
> - Last day to drop without a "W" on the college transcript: **[date]**
> - Last day to drop with a "W": **[date]**
> - After the "W" date a grade must be issued — there is no drop.
>
> It is the student's responsibility to drop through [College]'s system. Dropping the high school class is not the same as dropping the college class.

### 4. Grading scale (letter cutoffs)
Stats gives category weights (15 / 10 / 75) but never says what percentage earns what letter. The example publishes the full table. A DE course is issuing a college letter grade, so the cutoffs need to be stated.

> A 90–100% · B 80–89.99% · C 70–79.99% · D 60–69.99% · F 0–59.99%
>
> *(Add +/– rows only if the college reports them. Copy the example's finer scheme if [College] uses plus/minus grading.)*

### 5. Academic integrity beyond AI
Stats defines an AI policy but never defines plagiarism or cheating, states no consequence, and cites no policy. The example defines both terms, names the penalty, and cites the college's Administrative Procedure plus the student handbook.

> **Academic Integrity:** Plagiarism is presenting work, words, or ideas from an outside source as your own. Cheating is giving or receiving answers or information by any means not expressly permitted for that assignment. Either results in a zero on the assignment, a referral to administration, and — because this is a college course — a report under [College]'s academic honesty policy [AP ####], which can carry a failing course grade.

### 6. Term dates and where/when the class meets
The example states the term window ("June 1 to July 26, 2026") up front. Stats gives periods and a school year but no start/end dates and no office hours. Add the semester start/end and a stated help window (before school, lunch, after school, room number) — "Prep Period 1, 2" is not an office hour students can use.

### 7. Attendance rules that apply to the *college* grade
Stats covers tardies and detention — high school enforcement only. The example defines excessive absence, non-participation, and the drop consequence. Say what an absence pattern does to the college enrollment, even if the answer is "nothing, but the work is still due."

## Fix what's already in Stats

These are defects in the current document, independent of the comparison.

1. **The AI section is an uncleaned template.** It currently prints the Green, Yellow, and Red policies, each with an "Or" alternate — six mutually contradictory paragraphs — while line 2 declares the course "Yellow." A student reading it can't tell what's allowed, and the Green paragraph ("encourages students to explore AI for all assessments") directly contradicts the Yellow one you selected. Keep the declared Yellow paragraph and delete the rest. The example does exactly this: one policy, one paragraph.
2. **The cell phone policy is an empty table.** "Mobile Communication Devices | Consequences" has headers and no rows — confirmed in the `.docx` markup, so it's not a PDF conversion artifact. Either fill in the tiers and consequences or cut the section; the footnote about case-by-case exemptions is currently modifying nothing.
3. **Header says 2025 – 2026.** Stale if this is for the coming year.
4. **Typo, Individual Assessment:** "At the end of each after the Group Test and successful completion of the Practice Test…" — missing a word after "each."
5. **The Practice Test appears without introduction.** It's referenced as a gate to the Individual Test but never described in the grading section or the weights.
6. **The disclaimer says "verbal notice to the class."** The example puts changes in writing through the LMS. For a college-credit course, written notice is the safer standard.
7. **No course schedule or unit sequence.** Neither document has one inline — the example defers to the Canvas Course Summary. A DE course typically needs a unit-by-unit outline attached or linked, since it's the evidence the high school section covers the college course.
